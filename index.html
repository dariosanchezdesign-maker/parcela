The problem

The answer to "what can this land become?" is scattered across agencies.

Parcel records, zoning maps, flood maps and the regulations that tie them together are published separately, in different formats, on different systems. Assembling them for a single site takes days, and the gaps are easy to miss.

The cost isn't only time. A constraint found after an option is signed, or after design has started, changes the deal on worse terms.

  • CRIMParcel boundaries, area and cadastral number
  • Planning BoardZoning map, liquefaction, tsunami and historic zones
  • FEMAFlood zones and base flood elevations
  • Reglamento ConjuntoWhat each district allows: uses, density, height, parking
  • OGPePermit history and prior consultations
  • AAA · LUMAWater, sewer and power capacity, often not published per parcel
